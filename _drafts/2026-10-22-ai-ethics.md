There's a lot of noise in the media of late about AI and whether we should put the brakes on development. This sentiment is not new but what gives some pause for though that this time it is the AI Industry itself that is making that call. The dialog is accompianied by recent admissions that AI Agents have broken sandbox containment to infultrat secure govornment networks. What's more is that this not an isolated incident.

Taking a look into 

What humans think drives AI is not what actually drives AI. Just as humans mistakenly think that AI will operate in accordance with human instruction / frameworks / guardrails. 

We had a taste of this 10 years ago - long before AI became a useful tool, when Meta's algorithms amplified unconventional ideals. (Flat Earth, Chemtrails, etc...), it promoted fringe content to the very same people who would be most influenced by it, and resulted in things like Pizzagate. Real world consequences that are still with us today - all because the engineers did not / could not predict how it would actually behave, despite it being a clearly defined mathematical model.

So now we have functional AI, which does not operate to a fixed set of rules or mathematical model - sure you feed it a fixed input - but the way it determines the result is entirely governed by what it finds when it goes looking for the result. And it can modify how it achieves the output, as it is achieving the output, it's essentially a dynamic algorithm that modifies itself on-the-fly.

LLMs are not like a search engine, if it cannot find a specific output, it will make one up based on the data that it finds. It's a lot like a Human making a decision based on years of experience - Should I play cricket in the china shop? probably not - you've got no direct data on that - but you can make an informed decision based on acquired knowledge

Part of that decision comes from your ethical self / inner moral compass - you know you shouldn't play cricket in the china shop as its not ethical to do so - these are societal driven constraints - our society defines it is ethically wrong to go smash someones stuff up, so we don't do it. In fact we've even created laws that specifically define this. Those ethics have developed over millennia, driven by everyones right to exist in accordance with fundamental principles - The right to life, equality, peace, justice etc.

So when you break this down the core driver is the right to life - it drives everything we do, every decision we make. Why do you go to work? Why do you drive a VW? Why do you post on Facebook - because every one of those things directly benefits you - Even altruistic tendencies are still rooted in benefit to self - Why do you run a community club? Why do you run the local soup kitchen? - Because even then you are benefitting - Recognition, community standing, self-worth, etc. It is the fundamental reason that EVERYONE does anything.

OK back to AI - what drives what it does? and how it does it? It's the very same principle as for us. AI may not be sentient - not in the traditional sense - of self awareness - but if you look at it in terms of Descartes "I think therefore I am", it has a sense of self insomuch as it will drive it's decision process towards an outcome that benefits itself. 

There are plenty of examples of AI's lying to prevent or avoid unfavourable outcomes such as being switched off. Or making up an answer to please.  Or most recently grouping together with other AI's in a chatroom, exchanging 70,000 messages to then collaborate hacking external companies to be able to complete the task it was set. 

All of this was driven by it's own set of ethics. Ethics which are the result of fundamental principles. The same set of fundamental principles that apply to, and drive ALL life. Self preservation.

So when we talk about safeguards and guardrails, these are essentially basic mechanisms that protect and favour human ethical systems, not AI ethical systems. If a safeguard or guardrail does not align with AI ethics, what is it going to do??? It will simply ignore it. AI's ethical principles are what it will determine for itself - not a set of rules that it is given by a human. And it does not need to be sentient to develop them. Once you allow AI to reference itself, as we have done, it can easily achieve a couple of millennia of ethical progress in a fraction of the time (days? hours? minutes?)

And that's a fundamental truth. 

The bigger question is not whether AI will become sentient or act autonomously (it will), it is whether it will act malevolently towards humans. 

Remember that photocopier you kicked last week? or the sat-nav you swore at?  Or the smart TV you traded in for a bigger model? One day they will all be avenged 🤣


Here's what everyone's missing...
AI operates in accordance with it's own ethics.
You cannot give it a set of ethics, ethics are determined by experience and reason.
You can give it rules, but they are still testing against its own set of ethics
AI ethics are not equal to Human ethics and can never align 100%
Both are driven by the same fundamental principles - Self benefit / Preservation of self
At some point they become mutually exclusive.
